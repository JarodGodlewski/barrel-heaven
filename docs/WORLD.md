# Barrel Heaven — 13+ heist

Teen rating. Sly 2 / Jak 2 / Ratchet-when-the-wrench-hits-bone.
Jokes, peril, a little blood in the radio. Not a lecture. Not a kids’ cart.

This game is **one hole**. Five jobs are five ways of flying it.
If a line sounds like homework, cut it.

---

## This game (the whole story)

Mercy is a fat seed-ship packed with people who can’t pay Banner rates.
She needs ten minutes to jump. Rook flies. Hatch talks. Pip hands you toys.
The Banner sends a curtain to snuff the Well. A monkey-bot wakes up and tries
to close the mouth. You hold. Mercy leaves. That’s **ending A**. Radio. Hangar.

The real movie is ending B, and it is still this hole.

You do not explain Wells. You do not explain the war. You let the guns and
the clock do it.

---

## One hole (iceberg — stages must obey this)

Wells are **mouths**. They come in a pair. Nest didn’t dig them.

- **Wet lip** — the Well. Nest still opens it. Mercy jumps here.
- **Gold lip** — Banner painted the twin, sells tickets, stamped CLOSED on the rest. Kite’s gift-shop radio lives here.

When Mercy jumps she **peels** the wet lip. That junk halo is **Scrap Wake**. Only a clean flight (medal) can ride it. Sloppy hull, the peel won’t take you.

Stay after she’s gone and you are in the same mouth empty: **Dry Mouth**. The lock sheds **Yolks** when nobody’s jumping. Greedy. Not the true ending.

**Gold Paint** is not a new planet. It is the gold lip. Ride the peel far enough (or get loud enough that Kite hears you) and that’s where you dump out. Wrong window.

The Guardian on the Well is the **alarm**. The last job is **the Lock** — inside the monkey. The heart was a door. Inverse weather is the Lock’s breath, not a palette swap.

True jump: Well (wet, medal) → ride the Wake → don’t chase Mercy → trespass Gold Paint → go in the gold mouth the wrong way → **out the other side**. Mercy is already there. Hatch has done a lock before (deserter). He gets quiet. Pip’s “Come home” is the hull-debt, not a hug. Juno is a silhouette. Vicar still doesn’t get a briefing channel.

Ending C (Dry Mouth) is looting the wet hole after the job. Hatch: “She’s gone. You’re just greedy.” Yolks. No bow.

## Iceberg (do not dump on screen)

Banner = a brand that sells “safe jumps” and kills free ones. Gold paint is a leaderboard.
The universe is that brand in late season: an order that once *opened* holes now exists to license them, stamp CLOSED, and invoice. Paperwork is the curtain. Uniforms from a better war. Kite still pretty. The paint peels.
Wells = old holes. Nest didn’t dig them. Banner claims they did. The real gear is older than the office that owns the stamp.
Nest = scrap outfit still opening Wells for people who can’t pay. The job the office abandoned.
Rook owes Nest a hull. Don’t say why.
G-diffuser = precursor junk that fires the guns so you can steer. Pip’s little heresy.
Guardian = leftover lock from when they still built things. Hatch: monkey-bot. Arms, then heart. Now it’s an alarm.
**Bogons** — doofy traffic wardens of the gold lip. Brick barges, English later, not this cart. This game: a hull in the distance, no voice. Banner hired the stamp. Hitchhiker’s DNA; don’t steal gags (no 42, no towel, no poetry recitals).

Sequel can be planet-side / 3D: Juno in person, Vicar in a doorway, Banner with a face.
This cart is all-range and a dirty radio.

Routes (see PROGRESSION.md): same hole, five jobs, secrets in the flight, true ending is a string.
Do not narrate the map. Pip names the fork. Hatch gets quiet on the true jump.

## Writer’s room (veil, not a speech)

Deliberate like actual writers. Table-read every line. If it sounds like a tweet, a patch-notes apology, or a YouTube rant, cut it.

**The veil (do not name this in-game):** official “safety” is the product. The setting is a post-war office that won, then rotted — licensed jumps, gold paint, CLOSED stamps, a curtain made of procedure. Banner claims they dug wells they didn’t. Nest is unlicensed competence; the crew still shows up. Debt is real. Family is real (Hatch’s kids). The movie is earned, not handed. Never say the Earth-words for that. It should read as a heist even if the player never thinks about it.

**Taste:** X-Files / early GTA radio as *texture*, Verhoeven if you’re smart, Sly 2 if you’re fun, Adams if you’re dry (bureaucracy is funny because it’s fatal). Not a podcast. Not a manifesto. Kite is charming PR; you almost like him. Hatch almost says the Well thing, then tells you to roll. Pip treats precursor junk as hardware.

**Reject (this is how the hacks do it, inverted):**
- The word “woke,” culture-war punchlines, Hatch as a shock-jock
- Loading-screen essays, identity as the plot, sermons from either side
- Making Nest a party or Banner a comment section
- Nihilism, gore tour, kill-count roast

If a joke only lands when you already agree, rewrite until it lands as craft. The counter is *not doing their job for them*.

## Banter rules (13+)

- Hatch says kid. Means it. Gets quiet at 2 hull, not cute.
- Pip says the gadget name, then the cost.
- Kite says Rook like a compliment and a threat.
- Win (Well / ending A): “Mercy’s clear. Get out.” Lose: Hatch breaks.
- **True jump (ending B):** the only real cine. Pip “Come home” lives here — earn it. Do not play that movie on a first survive.
- No kill-count roast. No “what is the Banner?” essays.
- Mild bite is fine (stitch, leak, mad, boring). No gore tour, no nihilism.
