# Writer’s room — table read

Critique of `docs/WORLD.md` and the route string in `docs/PROGRESSION.md`.
Not a fan wiki. No in-game copy dump. No new radios. Hatch / Pip / Kite only.

If it sounds like a tweet, a patch-notes apology, or a talk-radio bit, it failed.
The veil stays in WORLD: Banner = licensed safety monopoly, Nest = unlicensed
competence, earned cine, no slogans.

This file argues cuts. It does not rewrite the bible. Chair calls at the end.

---

## How the veil is supposed to work

Official “safety” is the product. The setting is a post-war office that won, then
rotted. Licensed jumps, gold paint, CLOSED stamps, a curtain made of procedure.
Banner claims they dug wells they didn’t. Nest still shows up. Debt is real.
Family is real. The movie is earned.

Never say the Earth-words for that. It should read as a heist even if the player
never thinks about it.

**Taste check (WORLD already):** X-Files / early GTA radio as texture, Verhoeven
if you’re smart, Sly 2 if you’re fun, Adams if you’re dry. Not a podcast. Not a
manifesto. Kite is charming PR. Hatch almost says the Well thing, then tells you
to roll. Pip treats precursor junk as hardware.

## What the writers actually did (then Nest-clothes)

### Verhoeven — *Starship Troopers* (1997)

**Did.** He stopped reading Heinlein after two chapters and shot the movie *as*
Federation propaganda. First shot copies *Triumph of the Will*. Recruits look
into camera: “I’m doing my part.” Uniforms are the joke and the costume. He told
Empire he wanted the audience asking “Are these people crazy?” He also told
people he wanted it so obvious that everyone who gets it lives in torment next
to everyone who doesn’t. A lot of people didn’t.

**Nest-clothes.** Banner gold paint is allowed to be pretty. Kite is allowed to
be likable. Do not wink. Do not have Hatch mutter “fascism.” The risk Verhoeven
took — pretty uniforms, missed satire — is the risk we want. The failure mode is
Kite doing a bit. If Kite ever says “I’m doing my part,” cut the line and the
writer.

**Keep.** Paint that peels. Stamps. Gift-shop radio. Uniforms from a better war.

**Cut.** Any newsreel, any “do your part,” any recruitment poster as UI.

### Early GTA radio — *III* (2001) / *Vice City* (2002)

**Did.** Dan Houser (GameSpot, 2002): talk radio “adds very little in terms of
conventional gameplay” and a lot in making the city exist without you. Lazlow:
most listeners never call in. Colin Anderson (PC Gamer): the station only works
if it lives in the car. Chatterbox, ads, jingles — texture, not a quest giver.

**Nest-clothes.** We do not get a talk station. Three job radios. Kite is 1–2
times, which is already more than a civilian should hear from Banner. Hatch and
Pip are the crew channel, not Chatterbox. The Well should feel like it runs
whether Rook shows up or not: Mercy is already packing, the curtain is already
budgeted, Kite is already on shift.

**Keep.** World-without-you. Ads as *objects* (stamps, gold paint, a gift-shop
hold message) not as a second script.

**Cut.** A fourth voice. A call-in. A fake commercial read over the HUD. Any
line that exists to be quotable on a trailer.

### X-Files iceberg (1993–)

**Did.** The Cigarette Smoking Man is in the pilot and does not speak. The
Syndicate is never a briefing. “Musings of a Cigarette-Smoking Man” (S4E7) may
or may not be true — Frohike read a magazine. The show got worse when it
explained the colonisation calendar. The good trick was a man in a room who
already had the file.

**Nest-clothes.** Banner is the Syndicate as weather, not as a named cabal.
Kite is not CSM; Kite is the pretty face the office puts on the radio. The
office itself never calls. Vicar planned the jump and still doesn’t get a
channel — that’s the Mulder/Skinner split without the FBI. Juno is a silhouette
on B, which is the correct amount of sister.

**Keep.** Don’t dump the iceberg. Don’t confirm Rook’s hull-debt. Don’t name
the war.

**Cut.** Any “what is the Banner?” Any log that dates the Wells. Any scene that
proves Juno’s relationship in dialogue instead of Hatch going quiet.

### Sly 2 heist (2004)

**Did.** Fleming: rooftops feel thiefy because they are rooftops. Episodes are
setup verbs, then an operation. The gang is funny and the job is illegal and
nobody makes a speech about capitalism. T-rated blood stays in the joke, not
the camera.

**Nest-clothes.** The Well is the heist. True string is the operation. Banter
is tools and timing. If Hatch lectures Nest vs Banner he has become Bentley
with a blog.

**Keep.** “Hold the mouth ten minutes” as the job description. Setup secrets
as verbs (medal, hop, buoy, stay).

**Cut.** Mission-select flavour text that explains the Klaww Gang. We don’t
have a Klaww Gang. We have a hole.

### Jak II dark turn (2003)

**Did.** Rubin: revenge, guns, not a platformer kid. Haven’s police are in the
street. The city hates you before anyone says Baron. They kept the cartoon and
took the cute out. Enemies take more than one hit so combat is a situation.

**Nest-clothes.** 13+ is this, not a gore tour. Hatch says kid and means it.
At 2 hull he is not cute. Banner curtain is the patrol. Don’t write Jak’s
prison-prologue in radio.

**Keep.** Mean without a manifesto.

**Cut.** Revenge motivation for Rook. Rook is quiet and owes a hull. Flying is
the attitude. If we give him a revenge line we have started a different game.

### Children of Men (2006)

**Did.** Cuarón to the art department: not imagination, reference — Iraq,
Somalia, Sri Lanka, Northern Ireland, the Balkans, Chernobyl. “There’s a kind
of cinema I detest… exposition and explanations… a medium for lazy readers.”
Quietus ads, fertility-test announcements, cages on the bus, all in the
background of a long take. He still let Jasper explain the Fugees out loud and
it clunks. Lubezki: don’t make it pretty unnecessarily.

**Nest-clothes.** Banner late-season is reference, not concept art: peeling
gold, leftover alarms, uniforms that fit a better war, CLOSED stamped on a
mouth that still works. The Well should look like a workplace. Mercy should
look like a bus, not an ark in a painting — Titan energy in the silhouette,
Children of Men dirt on the hull.

**Keep.** Background weather. Long take on ending B, not on ending A.

**Cut.** Jasper. Any Hatch line that is “they hunt them like cockroaches.”
That’s the Earth-word. If we need the player to know Banner kills free jumps,
the curtain is already doing it.

### Alien’s Company (1979)

**Did.** Special Order 937. Crew expendable. “Building Better Worlds” is on
the crate. Ash is the company on the ship. The xenomorph is the animal; the
office is the villain. Nobody makes a speech about capitalism. The order is
the speech.

**Nest-clothes.** Banner’s product is safe jumps. The curtain is the order.
Guardian is leftover lock — a weapon that is now an alarm, which is the
Company leaving kit in the walls. Kite is not Ash. We do not get a traitor
on the radio this cart. Pip’s G-diffuser heresy is hardware, not a speech
about precursors.

**Keep.** Slogan as paint, never as subtitle. CLOSED stamp. Ticket window on
the gold lip.

**Cut.** A company-name stinger. A loading-screen mission statement. Ash.

### Le Carré’s office (*The Spy Who Came in from the Cold*, 1963; *Tinker Tailor*, 1974)

**Did.** The Circus used to be inspired amateurism over port. It became a
department: passes, fill out a slip, housekeepers, janitors. *Tinker Tailor*
is an office novel that happens to be about a mole. The war is over. The
building still has the good clock. People drink at lunch and remember when
the work mattered. Gloom is the point, not the gunplay. His editor asked for
less gloom. He kept it.

**Nest-clothes.** Banner won, then licensed the win. Hatch is the man who
left the office and still knows the lock. Deserter, not mole. We do not do a
mole plot. Uniforms from a better war is Le Carré, not a costume party.
Hatch getting quiet on the Lock is Smiley not explaining Control.

**Keep.** Procedure as the curtain. A gift-shop on the gold lip (the Circus
gift shop energy without the joke spelled out).

**Cut.** Codename soup. “The Circus.” A mole. Hatch explaining why he
deserted. That’s the hull-debt rule applied to Hatch.
