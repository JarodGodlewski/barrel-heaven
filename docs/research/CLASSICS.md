# Classics → Barrel Heaven

Studio research. Mechanics and structure, not plot. Wear Nest clothes.

Do not implement. Do not grow `main.gd`. Do not invent radio voices.
If a classic needed the strategy guide, say so — we radio the fork instead.

Read with `docs/WORLD.md`, `docs/PROGRESSION.md`, `docs/LEVEL1.md`.
This file does not rename stages. Five jobs, one hole.

**How to read an entry**

1. Cite
2. What they actually did
3. Steal / reject / Nest-clothes
4. Chair flag if it pokes the one-hole rule

## Routes & secrets

### Star Fox 64 (1997)

**Cite.** Nintendo EAD. Shigeru Miyamoto, Takaya Imamura. *Iwata Asks: Star Fox 64 3D*, vol. 1, “Star Fox 2 that Almost Was” (Miyamoto: all-range from the cancelled sequel, then hybridised with scrolling). Route count: 25 paths, Corneria → two Venoms — robot Andross via Bolse, true form via Area 6 / Venom 2 (Nintendo Power; Arwingpedia). Medals: hit-count plus all three wingmen alive (GameFAQs / Nintendo Power). Nintendo Power named one hard path “Miyamoto’s Route.” Warp rings in Meteo and Sector X are in-flight verbs, not a map menu.

**Did.** The campaign is a tiny route sheet, not a novel. Radio is the map: Peppy, Falco, Slippy tell you the fork while you fly it. Fake Andross is a full ending. The real one is a recipe you execute *that night*. Medals are a second game on top of surviving. The 25-path chart lived in Nintendo Power. Kids who didn’t have the guide took the easy planets and fought the dummy.

**Steal.** Routes as jobs on a map. Fake ending vs true form. Radio names the fork, then shuts up. Execute the string this sitting; unlocks are not a skip. Warp as a verb in the flight (rings, hop-pack).

**Reject.** Hit-count medals. Sixteen planets. A strategy-guide map. Wingmen you babysit. Any HUD that draws the 25-path tree.

**Nest-clothes.** The Well is Corneria — you always start wet. Ending A is Bolse/Venom 1: Mercy’s clear, hangar, no cine. Ending B is Area 6 → Venom 2: medal → Scrap Wake → Gold Paint → the Lock, one sitting. Pip says “Wake’s open.” Hatch does not explain the map. Medal is a *clean flight* (hull or Q — Chair, Q1), not 150 hits. If SF64 needed Nintendo Power, we spend that information on one radio line.

**Chair.** Kill-count medals are how SF64 made “ace” readable. WORLD already bans the roast. Do not sneak a hit counter back as “Star Fox authenticity.”

### Star Fox 2 (dev 1993–95, SNES Classic 2017)

**Cite.** Nintendo / Argonaut. Dylan Cuthbert, Katsuya Eguchi, Miyamoto. *Iwata Asks* (above): all-range was running on Super FX 2; robots morphing; cancelled when N64 was close. Nintendo Life, June 2017 (Cuthbert): Eguchi wanted a more “Rogue-like” structure; Miyamoto called Star Fox his test bed; original Star Fox stayed on rails because that was what Super FX 1 could do. Polygon, “Star Fox 2: By the numbers” (2017): zero traditional corridor levels. Kotaku, 2017: Nintendo posted the 1995 design docs / medal maps with the SNES Classic manual.

**Did.** The point of the sequel was not more rails. It was 360° fighting in zones, a map of threats, walkers, and a medal hunt buried in the stage. All-range was left out of the first game for hardware, then became the series’ real verb.

**Steal.** All-range *is* the game. The Well is a zone, not a tunnel into the mouth. Medal as a thing you do in the space, not a score screen.

**Reject.** Real-time strategy layer. Transforming walkers. A galactic map of incoming fleets. Randomised “every playthrough different” campaign (Eguchi’s itch). That wants a different cart.

**Nest-clothes.** Barrel Heaven is the all-range game SF2 was trying to be, in one hole. Hop and roll are how you use the zone. Rails are a camera lie we do not owe the player.

**Chair.** None. Already locked.

### OutRun (1986)

**Cite.** Sega AM2. Yu Suzuki. Eurogamer, 2015 (Suzuki: Cannonball Run, then a Europe drive when someone told him the American landscape doesn’t change). Retro Gamer interview: first stretch is traffic-free so a fresh 100-yen coin isn’t punished in three seconds; forks are left/right at the end of each stage. Original 1986 design docs (CEDEC 2019 / Reassembler): branching roads were in the plan before the Testarossa.

**Did.** Five stages. At each checkpoint the road splits in front of you. You can *see* the easier and harder fork. Fifteen courses, a binary tree, no menu. The fork is a steering decision at speed, not homework.

**Steal.** Forks you can see. The wet lip peeling into Scrap Wake should be a thing in the sky, not a pause-screen node. First minutes of The Well stay readable — don’t dump the curtain on second one.

**Reject.** A holiday postcard of new biomes every fork. OutRun’s joke is Europe changing every five minutes. One-hole rule: the gold lip is the same mouth painted. If a fork needs a new planet, it failed.

**Nest-clothes.** Mercy’s peel is the visible fork. Medal riders can take it. Sloppy hull, the road doesn’t open — that’s the OutRun miss, not a locked gate UI. Dry Mouth is staying on the road after the checkpoint. Gold Paint is the other colour of the same tarmac.

**Chair.** How visible is the peel? Geometry in the sky (OutRun) vs Pip’s one line (Star Fox radio). Both are legal. Pick one as the primary tell so we don’t do both and still confuse people.

### Super Mario 64 (1996)

**Cite.** Nintendo EAD. Miyamoto, Tezuka, Koizumi. Miyamoto wanted players to “create their own vision” because precise A-to-B jumps in 3D were a bastard (Mario Wiki; 25th-anniversary interviews). Courses are *hakoniwa* — box gardens, layers you notice when you look. Did You Know Gaming (Seth Everman): the team started with flagpoles, then swapped them for stars so you could wander like Miyamoto’s hamster. Each main course: six named missions plus a 100-coin star. Secret stars live in castle geometry (slide, wing cap light, mirror room), not a menu.

**Did.** A star is a *verb in the room*. Same garden, eight reasons to be there. The castle is a hole with paintings; secrets are in the architecture. You do not unlock “World 4.” You notice the light on the floor and look up.

**Steal.** Five jobs, one hole. Medal / hop-pack / off-cardinal buoy / stay / Kite-hears-you are stars in The Well, not new planets. Kit-door marks (Isaac grid, tiny) are the castle painting checkmarks.

**Reject.** A hub castle you walk. Paintings to other worlds — that *is* five planets. 120-star homework. 100-coin grind as a medal (that’s a kill-count in a clown wig).

**Nest-clothes.** The Well is Bob-omb Battlefield: first garden, several verbs, you can leave after one. Scrap Wake / Dry Mouth / Gold Paint / the Lock are the same mouth at different hours, not basement paintings. If a secret needs a text prompt, it isn’t a star.

**Chair.** None if we keep secrets as flight verbs. Breaks the one-hole rule the moment a “painting” loads a new skybox and we call it a planet.

### Metroid (1986) / Super Metroid (1994)

**Cite.** Nintendo R&D1. Yoshio Sakamoto. Retro Gamer (Sakamoto): *Alien* and Giger; they wanted backgrounds that felt alive, not “rows of blocks.” Super Metroid team interview (Metroid Database): story told with almost no text, “like a silent movie”; Samus’s face stays inside the helmet. Siliconera, 2011 (Sakamoto on Fusion): he put monologues in to help newcomers and still doesn’t know if that was right — “the true fun of Metroid lies in spending time searching for secret passages and getting yourself hopelessly lost.”

**Did.** The secret is a crack in the wall. Missiles and bombs are keys that live in your hands, not a journal. The map is something you draw with your face. When they explained it (Fusion), the designer flinched.

**Steal.** Secret as geometry. G-diffuser, hop, roll, Q are the missiles. The Lock’s heart-as-door is a Metroid wall that was always a door. Atmosphere over briefing. Hatch almost says the Well thing, then tells you to roll — that’s Sakamoto putting the helmet back on.

**Reject.** Getting lost as the loop. A 10:00 Well cannot be Brinstar. A pause-map with undiscovered rooms. Samus voiceover. Any “log entry” about precursor races.

**Nest-clothes.** Bombable blocks become: the off-cardinal buoy is *in* the arena, the peel is *in* the sky, the gold cache is a place you can sit. If it only exists on a route screen, it is not Metroid. Super Metroid needed a map; 1986 Metroid needed the strategy guide. We radio the fork.

**Chair.** Don’t add Chozo-style lore plaques to Nest hardware. G-diffuser is Pip’s wrench, not an artefact description.

### The Binding of Isaac (2011) / Rebirth (2014)

**Cite.** Edmund McMillen, Florian Himsl; Rebirth Nicalis. Polaroid unlocks after five Cathedral/Isaac clears; Negative after five Sheol/Satan. Both drop after Mom; pick one, the other vanishes (Rebirth wiki). Polaroid → Chest / ???. Negative → Dark Room / The Lamb. Completion marks: a post-it on the character door, one stamp per boss/path, Hard variant. True ending is a *sequence*, not “beat Mom.”

**Did.** Beating the obvious boss is ending A. The real movie is a recipe you learn across runs, then execute. The door shows what you’ve done. Polaroid vs Negative is a fork made of an item, not a cutscene choice.

**Steal.** Ending as a recipe. Marks on the kit door: Well / Wake / Mouth / Paint / Lock. Unlock once (node appears) vs execute tonight (Star Fox sitting). Dry Mouth is the greed path — Negative energy, not the true jump. Payoff lives on B only.

**Reject.** Twenty-four marks. A wiki of item synergies. Mutually exclusive Polaroid/Negative as our *only* fork — our true string is sequential, not a binary pickup. Mom-fight as a character-select essay.

**Nest-clothes.** Polaroid = medal (clean, wet, forward). Negative = stay/greedy (Dry Mouth, Yolks). You can take the greed door without ruining the cart; you cannot take it and also get the cine. Isaac players needed a spreadsheet for Dead God. We stamp five boxes on three hulls and stop.

**Chair.** Isaac lets you hold Polaroid *or* Negative. Our map lets Gold Paint be reached two ways (Kite hears you, *or* the true string). That’s a different shape. Confirm the two doors into the gold lip are both intended, and that the Kite door is “wrong window,” not a shortcut to B.

### Spelunky (2008 freeware / 2012 HD)

**Cite.** Derek Yu; Andy Hull on HD. GDC 2011 postmortem (Yu/Hull). Rock Paper Shotgun, 2016: Olmec is a push-block; mummy vomit is green blood; the ghost can die in lava — players found it. Yu on communal discovery (GDC / “Rediscovering the Mystery of Video Games”): Japanese arcade notebooks; you can bury a 1-in-1000 because the community will find it; dead-end corridors make the treasure land.

**Did.** Secrets live in the level’s physics, not a menu. The designer did not document the eggplant. The notebook was other players. Complexity is simple objects colliding.

**Steal.** Off-cardinal buoy, hop-pack, stay-after-Mercy: verbs in the arena. Communal “did you know you can ride the peel” is allowed. Dying to the wave is the teacher (PROGRESSION already).

**Reject.** 1-in-1000 buries. We are a $0.99 cart with one-finger touch. If it needs a wiki clip, Pip says the line. Don’t ship a ghost-in-lava and shrug.

**Nest-clothes.** Spelunky can afford mystery because death is a joke and the run is three minutes. The Well is ten. Radio the fork. Leave *one* thing undocumented if Chair wants a campfire (Bogon barge on Gold Paint — already locked as glimpse). That’s the eggplant. Not a third ending.

**Chair.** One undocumented glimpse max. Bogon barge is the candidate. Everything else that forks a job gets a Pip line.

## Horde / numbers

### Vampire Survivors (2021)

**Cite.** Luca Galante / poncle. Steam news, 24 Mar 2022 (Galante: Magic Survival is why VS exists; Serenade of Chaos asset pack). Character door prints Might, Cooldown, Area, MoveSpeed, Amount, Duration, Magnet, Max Health; PowerUps shop is tiny forever +stats; Golden Eggs add +1 random printed stat to *that* character, infinite sink; Inverse (Gracia’s Mirror) triples gold, doubles enemy HP, flips the map; Limit Break dumps extra levels into stats; Arcanas are timed rule-cards. Cappella Magna + Inverse is the late farm.

**Did.** Runs break because every toy multiplies the same short list of numbers. The door is a stat sheet with a silhouette. Meta (eggs, inverse, powerups) is a second game you unlock by doing verbs, not by reading.

**Steal.** The loop. Printed stats. Kit door. Yolk = Golden Egg, Pip’s word, Dry Mouth gated. Hangar +1s = PowerUps. Limit break after four hardpoints lv5. Heresies later = Arcanas, radio pick, not a pause shop. Inverse *math* as a Well modifier (PROGRESSION beat 5), not a new biome.

**Reject.** 20+ characters. Queen Sigma. A relic that *visually* inverts the stage (WORLD: inverse weather is the Lock’s breath, not a palette swap). Spreadsheet screens on day one. Naming anything “egg” in UI.

**Nest-clothes.** Three kits, then the galleon. Stats on the door, no lore page. Dying to the wave teaches; shop XP does not replace hop and roll. Inverse curtain = Gold Banner paint + faster `unlock_scale` after Dry Mouth + one Yolk. Same map, meaner math.

**Chair.** PROGRESSION “Inverse curtain” vs WORLD “inverse weather is the Lock’s breath.” Not a patch tonight. Are they the same weather, two names, or two beasts?

**Breaks one-hole?** VS Inverse-as-new-stage would. We already refused that.

### The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy (1978 radio / 1979 book)
